## Summary

The six windows of this first issue did not split. The five we could read, North America, Europe, Russia, China and the Middle East, lined up bearish on gold, bullish on the dollar and bullish on US yields, and gave the same reason: the hot October CPI and Fed officials talking up hikes. In the week 44 trial run, Europe had read the hikes as priced in, with neither the dollar nor yields having further to go, and split from China. This week ING wrote that the dollar is not to be fought near term, and that split was gone.

The regional difference was not in direction but in which driver came first. The North American outlets described real yields and a stronger dollar capping investment demand; Europe made the dollar's momentum itself the subject. The Chinese outlet declared a change in the trading logic, that geopolitical conflict no longer directly supports gold. The Middle Eastern outlet explained that higher yields had strengthened the dollar's appeal, and did not touch Gulf or US-Iran drivers at all. Russia had no gold article, and its dollar and rates pieces were rewrites of Reuters wires with no Russia-specific reading in them.

India had no article on gold, the rupee or the Fed across three sections and is published as unconfirmed. The Indian window is expected to be hard to read for tone; it will mean more once the physical layer, the gap between the domestic retail price and the international price, is added.

As this is the first issue there is no check of last week. From week 47, the readings logged here are judged mechanically against the week-on-week change in gold, the dollar index and the 10-year Treasury yield. Articles from the Friday US session are not included in this issue.
