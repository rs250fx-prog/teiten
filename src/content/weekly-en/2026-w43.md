## The core theme of the week

**Money fled from equities into Treasuries, but it did not go to gold. Gold was not the haven; it was on the side being sold to make cash.**

On 10/22 results from Meta and from Lam Research set off fresh worries about AI investment, and the Nasdaq fell 2.60%, its largest fall since June (Reuters). Money fled into Treasuries: the 10-year yield fell 17bp in a single day to 4.96%, the largest daily fall in more than 2 years, and broke below 5% (Yahoo Finance). On the week it went from 5.09% to 5.00% (−9bp) and the real yield from 2.74% to 2.66% (−8bp). Breakeven inflation (10-year) barely moved, from 2.35% to 2.34%, so **the fall in yields happened almost entirely on the real side** (the Treasury, FRED). The dollar index was close to flat as well, from 101.84 to around 101.75 (reading).

For gold this was a week with the tailwinds lined up. Even so gold fell 4.07%, from 4,165.61 to 3,995.88 dollars (reading), and on 10/23 it broke 4,000 dollars intraday to reach its lowest since July (Reuters, via FinanceFeeds). **The more the real yield fell, the more gold fell with it.** On 10/22, a day the real yield dropped 12bp, gold lost 2.81%, its largest fall of the week. Bloomberg and Reuters pointed to selling of precious metals to cover losses or margin on other assets (FinanceFeeds). What the previous week's issue wrote as the editor's assumption, selling to raise cash against losses in AI shares, was backed by reporting this week.

Set against the previous week's (week 42) scenarios, **the close matched the bear objective (4,000 after breaking 4,106) and went further, through 4,000 dollars.** Of the triggers, the acceleration of selling in AI-related shares and Brent clearing 110 dollars both happened. The VIX stayed in the 19s at the close and did not clear 22.31, and the 10-year breakeven did not rise to 2.4%. The chain was different too: the bear case saw the fall in nominal yields stopping, the decline in the real yield pausing and the dollar returning to the 102s, while in fact the real yield fell 8bp and the dollar never reached 102. **With rates and the dollar still a tailwind, selling to raise cash alone carried gold to the bear objective.** For a second week running, none of the three cases had allowed for a path in which gold falls while the tailwinds blow.

Behind that, oil kept rising on the attacks in the Middle East. Brent gained 6.2% on the week to 116.72 dollars, the highest settlement since April 29 (CNBC). The market has all but written off a rise at the October FOMC (a probability of about 5%, Bloomberg), but about 80% of a December rise is priced in (Reuters, via Kitco). **Higher oil keeps the view of a December rise alive and makes the tailwind of falling rates look temporary** (this includes the editor's assumption).

Money　AI-related shares and Japanese semiconductor shares → sold ／ Treasuries → bought (10-year −9bp) ／ Yen → bought ／ Oil → bought (Brent 116.72 dollars) ／ Gold → sold to raise cash, −4.1%

## Key events, reviewed

### 1. Fresh worries about AI investment, and a US 10-year below 5% (10/19 to 10/23)

The first half of the week began with buying back. On 10/19 the Philadelphia semiconductor index rose 1.6%, ending an eight-day losing run (Globe and Mail / Reuters), and on 10/21 the Nasdaq added 1.05% as results came in above expectations. After the close on 10/21 Meta reported July–September revenue of 64.6 billion dollars (up 26% year on year) and guided to 135 to 145 billion dollars of annual capital spending, and its shares rose about 3% after hours (TipRanks).

In the regular session on 10/22, however, Meta fell about 7% and Lam Research's results also disappointed (Reuters). The Nasdaq fell 694.00 points (−2.60%) and the S&P 500 1.21%, its largest fall since July, while the Dow managed only +0.02% (Kitco, Yahoo Finance). Tokyo sold AI-related names ahead of the US, and the Nikkei 225 fell 2.65% on 10/22. In the US on Friday the selling in AI-related names eased, but higher oil weighed: the Dow fell 1.23%, the S&P 500 0.52% and the Nasdaq 0.21%, all three indices down together (Yahoo Finance, CNBC).

→ The 10-year fell 17bp to 4.96% on 10/22 and ended the week at 5.00%, up 4bp (the Treasury). The probability of a rise at the October FOMC fell to about 5%, from 37% at the end of September and 15% earlier in the week on Bloomberg's calculation (Yahoo Finance). **About 80% of a December rise, however, stays priced in** (Reuters, via Kitco), so the buying in bonds was not a read on the end of rate rises but a flight from equities (this includes the editor's assumption).

### 2. Gold breaks $4,000: selling to raise cash inside a tailwind (10/19 to 10/23)

On the USAGOLD confirmed values, gold was 4,140.83 dollars on 10/19 (−0.59%), 4,124.85 on 10/20 (−0.39%), 4,140.75 on 10/21 (+0.39%) and 4,024.38 on 10/22 (−2.81%). For 10/23 the reading was 3,995.88 (−0.71%). Falling on 10/19, when the real yield rose 6bp, was a straightforward reaction, but gold also fell on 10/20 (−3bp) and on 10/22 (−12bp). **Of the five days, only two, 10/19 and 10/21, moved the way the real yield pointed.**

On 10/23 gold broke 4,000 dollars intraday. The low could not be confirmed from a source and is left unconfirmed. Reuters put it at the lowest since July 20, with silver, platinum and palladium all falling as well (Reuters, via FinanceFeeds). In a Kitco interview Jesse Colombo pointed to selling to raise cash alongside the selling in AI shares, and to caution ahead of the FOMC (10/22). → **Gold was treated not as a haven but as an asset that can be turned into cash.** This week's fall in yields came out of the flight from equities, and gold was sold to cover the losses in those same equities. That the tailwind and the selling pressure came from one event is the most awkward part of gold's week (this includes the editor's assumption). BTC also fell 0.6%, from 81,524 dollars (10/17 09:00 JST) to 81,057 dollars (10/24 09:04 JST), and broke 82,000 dollars on 10/22 (CoinGecko, Yahoo Finance).

## How money moved this week

**AI-related shares → into Treasuries and the yen ／ Oil → bought on the probability of an attack ／ Gold → the source of cash-raising sales even in a tailwind**

The first flow was **the flight from AI-related shares into Treasuries**. The Nasdaq ended the week down only 0.71%, but on 10/22 alone it fell 2.60%, erasing the first half's recovery. The Nikkei 225 fell 4.03% on the week, with semiconductors at the centre of the decline. The receiving side was Treasuries, and the longer the maturity the more was bought: the 30-year fell 10bp, the 10-year 9bp and the 2-year 5bp. In currencies the yen was bought: USD/JPY fell from 158.13 to 156.76 on 10/22 alone, and on the week from 157.99 to 156.81 (−0.74%, 17:00 New York, Yahoo). The dollar index was almost flat, so **as in the previous week, the haven was not the dollar but Treasuries and the yen.**

Money　AI-related shares and Japanese semiconductor shares → sold ／ Long-dated Treasuries → bought ／ Yen → bought ／ Dollar → flat

The second flow was **money into oil and into shares less exposed to the economy**. Brent rose 6.2% on the week and WTI (December contract) 4.3%. The Dow gained 0.41% for a third straight week, splitting from the Nasdaq's second straight fall. But when oil rose on Friday, transport shares were sold and the Dow lost 1.23% in a day, giving back most of the week's gain. **Until now higher oil had been absorbed by shifting money within the equity market; on Friday it began to turn into a weight on equities as a whole** (this includes the editor's assumption).

Money　Oil → bought (Brent 116.72 dollars) ／ Non-AI names in the Dow → bought in the first half of the week, sold on Friday

Third, **gold became the source of cash-raising sales.** The real yield fell 8bp, the dollar index was almost flat, and tension in the Middle East rose. None of the three axes was a headwind for gold, yet gold fell 4.07% and broke 4,000 dollars. Day by day, gold's large falls are concentrated on 10/22 (−2.81%), the same day the Nasdaq fell 2.60%. The previous week the shape was gold being sold a little on the days equities fell; this week it was sold heavily on the day equities fell. ## Looking to next week

### The event that matters most

**The FOMC on Wednesday 10/28, and the Alphabet and Microsoft results after the close the same day.** Most expect the FOMC to hold the policy rate at 3.75% to 4.00%; in a Reuters survey, 94 of 100 respondents looked for a hold (Newsquawk). It is the meeting after September's unanimous 0.25 point rise. The focus is how Chair Warsh addresses the December rise, about 80% priced in, at the press conference (Reuters, via Kitco). It falls a week before the midterm elections on 11/3, and Reuters reports that the market doubts a rise just before the vote.

The results put the capital spending that triggered the selling in Meta this week directly in question. Alphabet and Microsoft report on 10/28 and Amazon on 10/29, in a week when about a third of S&P 500 companies report. Capital spending by the hyperscalers is seen at about 800 billion dollars this year, and Reuters carried the view that the market is no longer giving companies spending such sums the benefit of the doubt (via Kitco). Intel, SanDisk, Eli Lilly, Exxon, Chevron and Caterpillar also report during the week.

On prices, September PCE and the first estimate of July–September GDP come on Thursday 10/29. Expectations are 3.6% year on year for PCE (3.4% in August), 3.1% at the core (3.0%) and an annualised 3.0% for GDP (Newsquawk). **PCE lands the day after the FOMC, so whether the hints from the press conference are backed up or undone will be seen within a day.**

### Domestic politics and policy

The focus is the Bank of Japan's Monetary Policy Meeting on 10/29 and 10/30 and its Outlook Report. The Bank only raised the policy rate to around 1.25% in September, and a hold is reported to be expected this time (TV Tokyo BIZ, TBS). TBS reported that the Bank is likely to judge that underlying inflation, the measure it watches, has reached 2%. National CPI for September, released on 10/23, showed the index excluding fresh food up 2.1% year on year, above 2% for the first time in nine months (Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications, Jiji Press). Tokyo-area CPI for October comes on the morning of 10/30, the day the meeting ends. Newsquawk reports that the market prices about 80% of a December rise.

USD/JPY went from 157.99 to 156.81 this week as US yields fell (17:00 New York). If the central banks of both countries end up holding while leaving a December rise on the table, the read on the rate differential will turn on the words at the two press conferences. We could not identify a domestic political factor that moved markets this week.

### Geopolitics

Attacks on tankers continue in the Strait of Hormuz, and a Saudi oil field and an airport were hit as well. There are reports that the US and Israel have drawn up plans to resume attacks on Iran after the midterm elections on 11/3 (Yahoo Finance), and President Trump again did not rule out operations on 10/23. The US ambassador to Turkey was reported as saying it could be as soon as this weekend (AFP, via Newsquawk). Israel holds a general election on 10/27, and the view is that whichever camp takes power, the hard line on Iran will continue (Newsquawk).

**If military action comes over the weekend, Monday starts with higher oil.** If Brent heads for 120 dollars, the chain of headwinds for gold starts to move: higher oil → higher expected inflation → more of a December rise priced in → the fall in the real yield stops. If instead progress in talks is reported, oil and expected inflation fall together and the tailwind of lower rates reaches gold more easily. The lesson of this week is that **gold was not bought even as geopolitical tension rose**, and we expect geopolitics to work through oil and rates rather than gold next week too.
