## The core theme of the week

**One election result moved money in two directions. Cash left crude because the war was ending, and it left AI-related shares because regulation was tightening. Unlike the previous week, gold did not end up on the side being turned into cash on the day equities plunged.**

The first half of the week began with oil. On 11/2 Iran leaned towards a diplomatic solution and WTI fell 7.36% to 96.46 (CNBC). When the Democrats secured a majority in both chambers of Congress in the midterm elections of 11/3, Vice President Vance posted that the reason for the defeat was the war, and the market read it as moving closer to the end of the Iran war (CNBC). WTI fell to 88.12 on 11/4, and on the week it lost 12.76%, from 104.12 to 90.83. Breakeven inflation (10-year) fell 11bp, from 2.34% to 2.23%. Gold jumped 3.39% on 11/4 and, on the USAGOLD reading, rose 5.04% from a confirmed 3,875.20 on 10/30 to 4,070.59, taking back the 4,000 level.

What changed the direction was 11/5. The view spread that a Democratic Congress would tighten regulation of data-centre construction and AI development, and the semiconductor index fell 12.25% and the Nasdaq 5.52%, entering a correction (Fox Business, CNBC). In Tokyo the next day, 11/6, the Nikkei 225 fell 3,643.13 points (5.96%) to 57,434.77, the third-largest fall on a closing basis (NHK). **Even so, gold rose slightly on 11/5, to a 4,087.97 reading.** On 10/30 lower equities had brought selling of gold and silver to raise cash; this week they did not.

Set against last week's (Week 44) scenarios, **the close reached the bull objective (through 3,910 to 4,000, then 4,068).** The route, however, was not the one assumed. One of the bull triggers, payrolls coming in below expectations, did not happen: Friday's report was 165,000, far above forecast. What worked was the midterms ending without major disorder and the plunge in oil, and the real yield rose to 2.63% rather than breaking below 2.6%. What lifted gold was not a tailwind from rates but the weakening of the inflation and rate-rise headwind that oil had been carrying. The selling in AI-related shares did not stop, so that condition was missed as well.

Money　Oil → sold (WTI −12.76%) ／ AI and semiconductor shares → sold (Nasdaq −3.37%, Nikkei 225 −9.25%) ／ Gold, silver and platinum → bought back (gold +5.04%, silver +5.51%) ／ Copper and BTC → sold along with equities

## Key events, reviewed

### 1. The Democrats take both chambers in the midterms and oil falls close to 13% (11/2 to 11/4)

On 11/2 oil plunged as Iran leaned towards a diplomatic solution, with WTI closing at 96.46 (−7.36%) and the January Brent contract at 104.77 (−7.63%) (CNBC). 11/3 was polling day: a US strike on Iran's Qeshm Island was reported, while Qatar said it would continue to mediate, and WTI slipped to 96.09 (CNBC).

In the midterm elections of 11/3 the Democrats secured a majority in both chambers of Congress (AP, CNBC). The market read it as moving closer to the end of the war with Iran, and on 11/4 WTI fell to 88.12 (−8.29%) and Brent to 94.62 (−8.67%), a third straight daily fall (CNBC). The dollar was sold against the euro and the dollar index fell to 101.51 on the reading. The Dow rose 3.03%, and defence and oil shares were sold.

From 11/5 attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz continued and crude recovered, with WTI closing Friday up 0.31 dollars at 90.83 and Brent up 0.03 dollars at 98.32. Reuters reported that Iran had sent a nuclear proposal to the US through Qatar, while data from the UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) show attacks on shipping continuing at close to a record pace (CNBC).

→ **The war premium has drained out of oil. But the attacks have not stopped, and the talks have not reached a formal agreement.** Speculative length points the same way: about 22,000 contracts as of 11/3, down 30% from the previous week.

### 2. Equities plunge on worries about AI regulation, and the Nikkei takes its third-largest points fall on record (11/5 to 11/6)

The US market on 11/5 lost the previous day's post-election gain in a single session. The view spread that a Democratic Congress would tighten regulation of data-centre construction and AI development, delaying capital spending on AI and making it more expensive (Fox Business, CNBC). The semiconductor index fell 12.25%, Nvidia 9.26%, the Nasdaq 5.52% and the S&P 500 4.36%, and the VIX jumped 69.66% to 28.18.

Tokyo fell across the board the next day, 11/6. The Nikkei 225 was down more than 4,000 points at one stage and closed 3,643.13 points lower (5.96%) at 57,434.77. The fall was the third-largest on a closing basis and took the index below 60,000 (NHK). On the week it lost 9.25% from 63,290.11, and the Nikkei VI rose to 44.60, 1.7 times the VIX.

The US recovered on Friday: the Nasdaq rose 1.09%, the S&P 500 0.31% and the Dow fell 0.46% (Yahoo Finance). Even so the S&P 500 posted its largest weekly fall since June, with technology and energy shares down 7% and 11% respectively on the week (Yahoo Finance). No specific regulatory bill has been introduced, and the new Congress sits from January.

→ **What was sold was the outlook for regulation, and there is no substance to it yet.** The US high-yield spread widened only 4bp as of 11/5, so selling has not spread to corporate credit. This was money fleeing within equities, not a risk-off move accompanied by credit stress.

### 3. Friday's payrolls come in at 165,000 and the view that the Fed raises rates in December returns (11/5 to 11/6)

The equity plunge of 11/5 took the probability of a December rise from 82.7% to 70.1% (CME FedWatch, CNBC) and the 2-year down 12bp to 4.46%. On the same day the Bank of England raised its policy rate by 0.25 point to 4.00%, and Governor Bailey indicated it would not be the last rise unless the conflict in the Middle East was resolved (FXStreet).

US October payrolls on 11/6 showed non-farm employment up 165,000 against the 65,000 expected. The unemployment rate fell from 4.2% to 4.1% and average hourly earnings rose 0.4% on the month against the 0.3% expected. August and September were revised up by a combined 84,000 (Kitco, Yahoo Finance). The 2-year rose about 6bp to 4.53% after the release and the dollar was bought (Kitco). Kitco wrote that the strength of the labour market had firmed the case for a December rise.

Gold fell to 4,050.95 (−0.89%) after the release (Kitco) but ended the week at 4,070.59 on the USAGOLD reading, holding the 4,000 level. In an evening piece Kitco wrote that gold had held the 4,000 level even after the strong payrolls report, and put the next test at next week's price data.

→ **Payrolls restored the rate-rise expectations that the fall in equities had cut.** Derived from fed funds futures, December prices 0.76 moves, slightly above the 0.70 of the previous week. Even with equities sharply lower on the week, the outlook for rates has not come down.

## How money moved this week

**Oil → sold on hopes for a ceasefire, with speculators stepping back ／ AI-related and Japanese shares → sold on worries about regulation ／ Gold and silver → selling to raise cash stops, and they recover the ground the lifted headwind gives back**

The first flow was **the war premium draining out of oil**. WTI fell 12.76% and breakeven inflation 11bp, and almost all of the 8bp fall in the US 10-year was the fall in expected inflation. The real yield rose 3bp instead. In the CFTC data, managed money's net length in crude fell every week from about 41,000 contracts on 10/13 to about 22,000 on 11/3, close to half. The gold/oil ratio rose sharply from 37.4 to 44.9: the chain of dearer oil, inflation and rate rises, gold's largest headwind, weakened.

The second flow was **the flight from AI-related and Japanese shares**. The capitulation in semiconductors on 11/5 was amplified in Tokyo the next day, and the Nikkei 225 fell 9.25% on the week, close to 3 times the Nasdaq's 3.37%. Copper fell 3.72% and BTC 2.36%, sold along with equities, and BTC spot ETFs saw 1.282 billion dollars of outflows on the week. The gold/copper ratio rising from 604 to 655 also shows the equity plunge showing up in copper as concern about the outlook for growth. Even so the US high-yield spread widened only 4bp, and the MOVE index fell 7.42%, so bond volatility came down. **This was selling within equities, not tension across the financial system.**

Third, **the selling of gold and silver to raise cash stopped**. The gold/silver ratio fell slightly from 79.4 to 78.7, and silver recovered at close to the same rate as gold the ground it had been oversold the previous week. Speculative net length as of 11/3 was about 104,000 contracts in gold, a small fall, and about 4,000 contracts in silver, a small rise. This was not buying that builds positions but a recovery once the selling stopped. Over the last 8 weeks the correlation of gold with the real yield is −0.01 and with the dollar index +0.06, close to zero, while with the Nasdaq it is −0.32: **this week gold moved the opposite way to equities.** Derived from fed funds futures, December prices 0.76 moves and January a further 0.20, so the outlook for rate rises has not come down. Gold's gain was not a retreat in rate-rise expectations but the removal of the headwind from oil.

→ In summary: the Democratic victory worked on oil as hope for a ceasefire and on AI-related shares as worry about regulation. Oil drained out, expected inflation fell, and gold returned above 4,000 without being turned into cash even in the equity plunge. The headwinds that remain are the December rate-rise expectations that payrolls restored, and a real yield that is not falling.

## Looking to next week

### The event that matters most

The heaviest item is **US October CPI on 11/10**. The headline is expected at 0.3% on the month (0.6% last time) and the core at 0.2% (0.3% last time), and no year-on-year figure will be published this time (Newsquawk). The strength of payrolls has December priced at 0.76 moves on our calculation; a core above expectations brings that close to certain, and one below cuts again the expectations the fall in equities had already trimmed.

US October PPI follows on 11/13, expected at 0.4% on the month (1.3% last time) and 0.3% at the core (Newsquawk). The US bond market is closed on 11/11 for Veterans Day, so equities and the dollar take the first reaction to CPI.

### Domestic politics and policy

The Bank of Japan's summary of opinions from the October meeting is released on 11/10 (Newsquawk). The minutes of the September meeting, published on 11/5, showed members united on continuing to raise rates, and the yen was bought (FXStreet). If the summary shows many voices in favour of a December rise, it becomes a reason to buy the yen as the rate differential between Japan and the US narrows.

Japanese equities come straight off the third-largest points fall on record on 11/6, and the interim results of Mitsubishi UFJ, Sumitomo Mitsui and Mizuho land on 11/13 (StockTitan). How bank shares are received after the plunge will show whether the Nikkei 225's fall stays in semiconductors or spreads to financials.

### Geopolitics

Iran is reported to have sent a nuclear proposal to the US through Qatar, but no formal agreement has been announced. Attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz were reported again on 11/5, and UKMTO data show attacks on shipping continuing at close to a record pace (CNBC). Oil has given up its war premium on hope that the war is ending, but the attacks themselves have not stopped.

**If the talks move forward, this week's tailwind for gold continues: cheaper oil, then lower expected inflation, then a retreat in rate-rise expectations.** If instead the talks break down and attacks increase, what drained out this week returns as it was, and Brent goes back above 100. The US EIA's Short-Term Energy Outlook on 11/10, the OPEC monthly report on 11/11 and the IEA monthly report on 11/13 will show the view on supply in turn.
