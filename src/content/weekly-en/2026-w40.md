## The core theme of the week

**It was a week in which the outlook for the policy rate and long-term yields moved in exactly opposite directions.**

At the start of the week an October rate rise was priced at a probability of about 70%. August PCE on 9/30 came in at 3.4% on the year against 3.7% expected, and the September jobs report on 10/2 was weak, with a gain of 29 thousand and unemployment at 4.2%. That probability fell to around 20%. Vice Chair Jefferson and New York Fed President Williams have also indicated no hurry to raise rates further.

Yet the US 10-year yield, 5.17% the previous Friday, reached 5.344% intraday on 10/1, the highest since 2002, and even on the day of the jobs report it fell as far as 5.175% before closing at 5.28%. On the week that is up 11bp. The 30-year ended the week near 5.62%. **Even as the pricing of monetary policy loosened, the cost of long-term money did not fall.**

Broken down with FRED's confirmed values, between 9/25 and 10/1 the nominal 10-year rose 7bp, the real yield (10-year TIPS) went from 2.83% to 2.88%, up 5bp, and expected inflation (10-year BEI) from 2.34% to 2.36%, up 2bp. **Most of the rise happened on the real yield side.** For gold, which pays no interest, that is the hardest combination.

Set against last week's (Week 39) scenarios, the bull trigger, PCE and the jobs report both slowing, happened almost exactly as written. Even so, gold landed on the bear objective, a break of 4,230. **The starting point was right and the path was wrong.** The assumption that a retreat in expectations for a rate rise leads to lower long-term yields did not hold this week.

Money　Pricing of the short rate → retreats ／ Long bonds → selling continues → the dollar

## Key events, reviewed

### 1. Oil swings both ways, and Trump rejects Iran's proposal (9/28 to 10/2)

Oil changed direction with each piece of news. On 9/28 President Trump rejected Iran's proposal on reopening the Strait of Hormuz, and Brent rebounded into the 105 area. On 9/29 Saudi Arabia resumed loadings at Yanbu on the Red Sea and WTI fell 3.5% to 89.38. On 10/1, reports that the US would send a third carrier strike group to the Middle East, together with China halting fuel exports, took WTI back up 2.7% to 92.87. On Friday the G7 announced the release of 100 million barrels of crude and diesel reserves over four months, and WTI touched 88.06, the lowest in a month, before settling at 91.11.

→ WTI was down about 1.4% on the week, and Brent, on the December contract, was broadly flat at 102.25. **Oil swung without changing level, but each swing dragged US yields and gold with it.** On 9/28, the day oil rebounded, gold broke the 4,230 dollar neckline.

### 2. PCE and the jobs report break expectations for a rate rise, while long-term yields set a new high (9/30 to 10/2)

August PCE on 9/30 was 3.4% on the year with the core at 3.0%, both below expectations. ISM manufacturing on 10/1 kept expanding at 54.5, but its prices index jumped 6.8 points to 77.9. The September jobs report on 10/2 showed a gain of 29 thousand, revisions that took July and August down by 60 thousand combined, unemployment at 4.2%, and average hourly earnings up 3.0% on the year, the slowest rise since May 2021.

→ The pricing of an October rate rise fell to around 20% (16% to 22% on CME FedWatch-based measures, with differences by source), but December remains at about 80%. **The rate rise did not disappear; it moved back.** And long bonds, on both the PCE day and the jobs day, were bought right after the release and then sold again. The 10-year reached 5.344% intraday on 10/1 and the 30-year 5.693%.

### 3. Gold breaks its neckline, and equities rebound led by semiconductors (9/28 to 10/2)

Gold broke the 4,230 dollar neckline on 9/28 and fell to 4,110.87, the lowest since 8/5. On 9/30 it recovered to 4,219 right after PCE but closed at 4,155.93, ending September down 6.7% on the month. On Friday it moved into the 4,200s on the jobs report, then was pushed back to around 4,140 as long-term yields recovered.

Equities fell three days running from Monday to Wednesday, after which Micron's results on 10/1 and Nvidia's record high on Friday put semiconductors at the head of the rebound. On Friday the S&P 500 rose 0.73% and the Nasdaq Composite 1.2%. On the week only the Nasdaq rose, while the Dow lagged at a fall of 1.3%. The Nikkei 225 was also lifted by semiconductors, up 3.30% on 10/1, and closed the week up 2.9%.

→ **In the same week of rising yields, gold was handed its tailwind, the retreat in expectations for a rate rise, twice and could not hold either, while semiconductors were bought without reference to yields.** The sorting of money has continued from the previous week.

## How money moved this week

**Pricing of the short rate retreats → long bonds keep being sold → the dollar ／ Gold → the dollar and semiconductors**

The first flow was **the separation of the short end from the long end**. PCE and the jobs report took the pricing of an October rate rise from 70% to 20%, and the 2-year yield fell from 4.92% on 9/28 to 4.78% on 10/1 (FRED). The 10-year, meanwhile, rose from 5.17% to 5.28% and the 30-year into the 5.6s. For only the long end to be sold while the outlook for the policy rate falls suggests that a term premium, and worries about fiscal supply and demand, are being added to long bonds (this includes the editor's assumption). That is why next week's 10-year and 30-year auctions are being watched.

Money　Short-dated bonds → bought ／ Long bonds → sold

The second flow was **money moving from gold into the dollar**. The dollar index rose from 101.03 the previous Friday into the 102s on 10/1, and even after falling back on Friday's jobs report it was up about 0.9% on the week, a third straight weekly gain. On 10/1 the dollar rose even on a day when US yields fell, because the Bank of Japan's Summary of Opinions from the September meeting was less hawkish than hoped and the yen was sold into the 158s. For gold it was a week in which both headwinds, the real yield (+5bp) and the dollar (+0.9%), strengthened together, and gold fell about 3.5%.

Money　Gold → the dollar ／ Yen → the dollar

Third, **within equities the concentration out of cyclicals and financials into semiconductors continued.** The Dow was down 1.3% on the week and the S&P 500 down 0.3%, against the Nasdaq Composite up 0.5% and the Nikkei 225 up 2.9%. What split the indices was semiconductors: Micron and Nvidia. The Russell 2000 also recovered 0.9% on Friday, but taken across the week the division has not changed from the previous week: the pain of higher yields sits with cyclicals, and the benefit of AI demand with semiconductors.

Money　Cyclicals and financials → semiconductors and AI

Crypto is worth recording too. BTC was sold to around 83,000 dollars on 9/28, then reached 84,842 on 10/1 and recovered into the 85,000s on Friday after the jobs report. Spot ETFs saw about 149 million dollars of outflows on 9/30 and about 103 million dollars of inflows on 10/1, a changeable direction (CoinTelegraph; daily provisional figures differ by source). **Where gold could not turn the retreat in rate expectations into buying, BTC responded straightforwardly to the fall in yields.** Friday's close and the weekly change, however, could not be confirmed.

→ In summary: in a week when expectations for a rate rise retreated sharply, long-term yields and the dollar rose and gold fell. **What sets the ceiling on asset prices is not the outlook for the policy rate but the cost of long-term money and the dollar.**

## Looking to next week

### The event that matters most

**The FOMC minutes on Wednesday 10/7, the 10-year auction the same day, and the 30-year auction on Thursday 10/8.** The minutes record the 9/15 to 9/16 meeting and do not reflect the PCE and jobs data that followed. What to read in them is how many at that meeting were in no hurry to raise rates. Since the lead role this week belonged to long-term yields, though, **demand at the auctions tests the direction of the 10-year and the 30-year more directly than the minutes do.**

ISM non-manufacturing on Monday 10/5 turns on the services prices index. After the manufacturing prices index jumped to 77.9, the same move on the services side would harden the pricing of a December rate rise (about 80%). At the University of Michigan consumer sentiment survey (preliminary) on Friday 10/9, the one-year expected inflation rate is what to watch. The real test for prices is the CPI the following week, on 10/14.

### Domestic politics and policy

The focus is the timing of a Bank of Japan rate rise and how far the yen can fall. This week the Bank's Summary of Opinions on 10/1 was less hawkish than hoped and the yen was sold into the 158s, and on Friday core CPI for the Tokyo area accelerated to 2.7% on the year, taking the yen slightly stronger. The Japanese 10-year was at 3.092% on 10/1 (Ministry of Finance), its highest in 30 years, but **what moved the yen this week was not the rate differential: it was what the Bank said.** The next Bank of Japan meeting is on 10/30, and the question of October or December continues.

The official pushback against a weak yen that pulled it back about 1% the previous week showed no visible effect this week. Any renewed approach to the upper 158s would make the frequency and the strength of those remarks material again.

### Geopolitics

Talks between the US and Iran over the Strait of Hormuz have stalled since Trump rejected Iran's proposal on 9/28. At the same time the US is said to be sending a third carrier strike group to the Middle East, and the G7 moved to hold down fuel prices with a release from reserves. **Supply measures, the recovery in Saudi exports and the G7 release, are in a tug of war with military tension, and for oil the size of the swings matters more than the level.**

This week's experience was that a rebound in oil pushed yields up through inflation and expectations for a rate rise, and worked as a headwind for gold (9/28). If the talks progress and the premium in oil is peeled away, one weight on long-term yields comes off; if tension rises, the chain of dearer oil, then higher long-term yields, then cheaper gold moves again. OPEC+ is expected to leave its November production target unchanged, so the material lies less in the decision on volumes than in passage through the strait and insurance.

### Nikkei and the yen

The Nikkei 225 ended the week up 2.9% at 68,309 yen. On 10/1 it rose 3.30% to 68,956 yen, led by semiconductors, and on Friday it fell 647 yen as profits were taken at those highs. The Nikkei VI was 22.92 on 10/1 and jumped into the 27s on the morning of 10/2: gains and caution have gone on sitting side by side, as they did the previous week.

Next week the focus is which prevails, US long-term yields (the auctions) or strength in semiconductor shares, and whether a yen around 158 keeps supporting exporters.

Expected range: high 69,500 / low 66,700

The range is the editor's assumption. The upper bound marks a clear break of the 10/1 high (68,995 yen), and the lower bound the case where the gains are given back to the level before the surge of 10/1 (the 9/30 close of 66,753 yen).
