## The core theme of the week

**It was a week in which selling in AI shares, rather than prices coming in above expectations, moved the bond market. Money went from equities into Treasuries and energy, and gold could not join that flow.**

US September CPI on 10/14 rose 3.7% year on year at the headline and 2.6% at the core, both 0.1 point above expectations. PPI the next day, 10/15, rose 6.3% year on year (5.3% expected), the fastest in four and a half years, and retail sales were strong at 0.8%. By the textbook, yields rise. Instead the US 10-year yield, after reaching 5.27% intraday on CPI day, fell for 4 business days running to 5.19%, 5.10% and 5.09%, down 15bp on the week from 5.24%. The real yield fell 17bp from 2.91% to 2.74%, while breakeven inflation (10-year BEI) actually rose from 2.33% to 2.35% (Treasury, FRED). **The whole decline in yields happened on the real side.**

Behind that, money left equities. The Nasdaq Composite fell 4.48% on the week and the Nikkei 225 5.37%. The reasons to sell changed by the day and piled up: the report on memory from China's CXMT (10/12), the fall in AI-related shares after CPI together with worries about CoreWeave's bonds (10/14), and on Friday doubts about whether AI investment pays. Meanwhile the Dow rose 0.67%, healthcare set a record high, and energy was bought on costlier crude. **Within equities too, money moved from AI-related names into sectors less exposed to the economy, and into energy.**

Set against last week's scenarios (week 41), almost every trigger in the bear case happened. CPI came in high at 3.7%, PPI was strong, and Brent cleared 105 dollars. On 10/14 gold's confirmed value of 4,105.85 broke the 4,111 that had marked the bear line. The chain, however, broke part way. The bear case looked for the US 10-year to clear 5.36%; it fell to 5.09% instead, and gold recovered to 4,171 on 10/15. **The close landed inside the base range (4,111 to 4,230), but the route it took matched none of the three cases.** None of them had allowed for yields falling while prices ran strong.

On top of that, in a week when the real yield fell 17bp and the dollar index around 0.4%, gold ended down 0.7%. **Gold's two tailwinds, rates and the dollar, blew at the same time, and gold still did not rise.** That is the heaviest fact of the week.

Money　AI-related shares → sold ／ Treasuries → bought (10-year −15bp) ／ Crude and energy shares → bought ／ Gold → flat even with the tailwinds

## Key events, reviewed

### 1. CPI and PPI above expectations, and the long yield that did not rise (10/14 to 10/15)

US September CPI rose 0.6% on the month and 3.7% year on year at the headline (3.4% in August, 3.6% expected), and 0.3% on the month and 2.6% year on year at the core (2.5% expected). Gasoline, up 9.1%, accounted for more than half of the monthly rise (BLS). PPI on 10/15 rose 1.3% on the month and 6.3% year on year at the headline, with the core up 5.0% year on year (4.4% expected): the rise in prices had spread from energy into goods and services.

The bond market, though, took the weakness in the details over the strong headlines. Retail sales beat at 0.8%, but the control group, which is closest to personal consumption in GDP, managed only 0.1% (0.3% expected), and the Philadelphia Fed's business index fell from 37.8 to 20.9. On 10/14 only the 2-year rose, to 4.82%, while the 10-year closed at 5.19%, lower than the day before. On 10/15 the 2-year fell too, to 4.77%, and the 10-year lost 9bp in a day to 5.10%. On Friday Fed Chair Warsh told an IMF meeting that he does not decide the outcome of a meeting in advance, and gave no hint of a further rise (Yahoo Finance).

→ The probability of a hold at the October FOMC (10/27 to 28) is 87% (CME FedWatch, Reuters), and the pricing of an October rise, 19% at the previous weekend, fell to around 15% (Bloomberg, via Yahoo Finance). **In a week when inflation data came in above expectations, the pricing of a rate rise receded.** The market has begun to read the path by which higher prices eat into consumption and sentiment before it reads prices themselves (this includes the editor's assumption).

### 2. Selling in AI-related shares, and Tokyo's plunge a day behind (10/12 to 10/16)

The selling had started at the beginning of the week. On 10/12 a report on a new memory design from China's CXMT sent Micron and SK Hynix down more than 3%, and on 10/13 Korea's KOSPI fell about 5.5% (Korea Times). On CPI day 10/14 the Nasdaq fell 1.88% and CoreWeave 15.46%, as credit worries around AI piled on top of worries about rising yields. Tokyo took that selling a day later: on 10/15 the Nikkei 225 fell 4.90% to 64,425.95 yen and Advantest 14.96%. The Nikkei VI jumped to 36.13.

The US steadied on 10/15 and Tokyo rebounded 898 yen on 10/16, its first gain in 7 days, but the recovery did not reach 30% of the previous day's fall (Zaikei Shimbun). Then the selling resumed in the US on Friday. On doubts about whether AI investment pays, the Nasdaq fell 2.14%, Intel 5.79% and CoreWeave 11.91%, with semiconductors and software leading the decline (AP, Yahoo Finance). The S&P 500 and the Nasdaq closed at the week's lows (Investrade).

### 3. Tension in the Strait of Hormuz, and 5 straight daily gains in crude (10/12 to 10/16)

WTI (November contract) rose for 5 days running, to 93.74, 94.14, 95.09, 97.97 and 101.41 dollars, up 10.4% on the week. Brent (December contract) settled on Friday at 109.86 dollars, up 3.72%, and touched 110 dollars intraday, the highest since May (Xinhua). Iran is reported to be stepping up attacks in the Strait of Hormuz in response to the US blockade of its ports and the tighter sanctions of September, and Eurasia Group raised its probability of the conflict intensifying from 35% to 65% (Rigzone). Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, and attacks on Russian and Ukrainian energy facilities, also continue.

Steps on the supply side appeared as well. On Friday the US offered up to 17 million barrels from its strategic petroleum reserve, and the IEA agreed on a release of more than 20 million barrels of diesel (10/15). Rigzone reports that the volume of crude passing through the strait exceeded 100 million barrels last week, close to its pre-war level. Prices dipped on Friday on a report that Iran was open to talks, but were bought back at once (Investrade).

→ **Supply volumes are coming back, and yet the price rose.** What crude is reacting to is not the volume in front of it but the probability that attacks intensify (this includes the editor's assumption). The rise in breakeven inflation (10-year BEI) from 2.33% to 2.35% looks to be down to these higher crude prices. The rise in expected inflation partly cancelled out the fall in nominal yields.

## How money moved this week

**AI-related shares → into Treasuries and defensive shares ／ Crude → bought on the probability of conflict ／ Gold → selling to raise cash cancels the tailwinds from rates and the dollar**

The first flow was **the move from AI-related shares into Treasuries**. In a week when the Nasdaq fell 4.48% and the Nikkei 225 5.37%, the US 10-year fell 15bp and the 30-year 17bp: the longer the maturity, the more it was bought. The 2-year fell only 6bp, and the gap between the 2-year and the 10-year narrowed from 44bp to 35bp. Long bonds being bought in a week when CPI and PPI came in above expectations looks to be because they took in the money leaving equities (this includes the editor's assumption). The dollar index turned from the previous four straight weekly gains to a fall of around 0.4% on the week, and Reuters reported that the selling in AI shares brought no flight-to-safety buying into the dollar. **This week's refuge was Treasuries, not the dollar.**

Money　AI-related shares and Japanese semiconductor shares → sold ／ Long US Treasuries → bought ／ Dollar → sold

The second flow was **buying in crude and energy shares**. WTI rose 10.4% on the week and Brent 4.9%. On 10/12 the energy sector in the S&P 500 rose 1.43% and supported the index (Investrade), and healthcare set a record high on 10/15. Part of the money that left equities moved into assets that hold up against higher prices. Costlier crude supported expected inflation and concentrated the fall in nominal yields on the real yield.

Money　Crude → bought (WTI at 101.41 dollars) ／ Energy and healthcare shares → bought ／ Expected inflation → slightly higher (2.35%)

Third, **gold did not rise even with the tailwinds.** The real yield fell 17bp and the dollar index around 0.4%, both moving in gold's favour. Even so gold fell 0.7%, from 4,194.61 dollars to 4,165.58. Day by day, gold's large moves were on 10/14 (−55 dollars) and 10/15 (+65 dollars), responding straightforwardly to a stronger dollar on 10/14 and to a weaker dollar and the lower real yield on 10/15. **It is not that gold fails to respond to rates and the dollar: on the other days it kept being sold a little, in the same direction as equities.** Selling to cover losses in AI shares may have reached gold (the editor's assumption). BTC fell 1.2%, from 82,542 dollars (10/10 09:03 JST) to 81,528 dollars (10/17 09:04 JST), so the two alternative assets were weak together (CoinGecko).

Money　Gold → down 0.7% even with the tailwinds ／ BTC → sold (−1.2%)

→ In summary: prices above expectations did not push yields up, and the money that left AI shares went into Treasuries and crude. **The tailwinds from rates and the dollar did blow, but they were cancelled out by selling to raise cash in a falling equity market, and gold stayed boxed in between 4,106 and 4,172 dollars.** Whether the selling in equities stops next week decides whether the tailwind reaches gold.

### The event that matters most

**Tesla and Meta Platforms report on Wednesday 10/21.** This week's selling in equities centred on doubts about whether AI investment pays, and next week is the first in which companies answer that doubt with their own numbers. Forecasts put Meta's July–September earnings per share at 6.11 dollars (−15.7% year on year), revenue at 63.3 billion dollars (+23.5%) and capital spending at 39.7 billion dollars (against 18.8 billion a year earlier), with Tesla's earnings per share at 0.43 dollars and revenue at 27.9 billion dollars (CMC Markets). **Capital spending is expected to more than double from a year earlier while profit falls.** Whether the market accepts that combination is the question. Semiconductor and IT results from Texas Instruments, IBM and Lam Research follow in the same week (CMC Markets).

On prices and sentiment, flash October PMIs for Japan, the US and Europe, together with the US University of Michigan consumer sentiment index, come on Friday 10/23. The bond market this week responded to weakness in consumption and sentiment rather than strength in prices, so the question is whether the PMIs support that same reading. China's July–September GDP and September retail sales on Monday 10/19 will shape risk appetite at the start of the week. The FOMC (10/27 to 28) and PCE (10/29) fall the week after.

### Domestic politics and policy

The focus is the Bank of Japan's meeting on 10/29 to 30 and the September nationwide CPI on Friday 10/23. In remarks on 10/16, read out by Deputy Governor Uchida, Governor Ueda said the bank would raise the policy rate and adjust the degree of easing in line with the economy and prices (Kyodo News), and that the pace of rate rises would be considered with weight on keeping prices stable at around 2% (NHK). Some reports, on the other hand, see a hold as likely at the end-of-month meeting (Minkabu). A strong nationwide CPI would feed the argument over October against December.

USD/JPY moved between 157.9 and 158.5 yen this week and did not reach 160. Lower US yields have made the upside heavy. We could not confirm any domestic political development that moved the market this week.

### Geopolitics

In the Strait of Hormuz, several tankers and container ships are reported to have caught fire or sunk this week (Yahoo Finance), with Iran stepping up attacks in response to the US blockade of its ports. Eurasia Group raised its probability of the conflict intensifying to 65%, and Trump did not rule out a large-scale attack after the midterm elections on November 3 (Rigzone). The previous week his post saying there would be no attack until the midterms pushed the risk of military action back, but **this week the risk after the election began to enter prices.**

At the same time, crude passing through the strait is approaching its pre-war level, and a release from the US strategic reserve has been offered. With volumes returning while only the probability of attack rises, crude is moving on probability rather than volume. If Brent clears 110 dollars decisively, the chain of headwinds for gold starts to turn: costlier crude → higher expected inflation → the fall in the real yield stops. If reports of talks with Iran take firm shape, crude and expected inflation fall together instead.

### Nikkei and the yen

The Nikkei 225 ended the week down 5.37% at 65,324 yen. It fell for 6 days running through 10/15, plunging 4.90% that day, and rebounded 898 yen on Friday, though the recovery did not reach 30% of the fall. The decline centred on high-priced semiconductor shares: on 10/15 Advantest fell 14.96%, the largest drop in the Nikkei 225. The Nikkei VI fell from 36.13 to 32.17, still above the 30 area of the previous week.

Tokyo starts next week by taking Friday's 2.14% fall in the Nasdaq on Monday. The Tesla and Meta results on 10/21 feed into Tokyo on Thursday 10/22, and the domestic CPI comes on 10/23. The questions are whether the selling in AI-related shares in the US stops, and whether a currency whose upside has grown heavy ahead of 160 yen supports exporters.

Expected range: high 67,800 / low 63,500
