## The core theme of the week

**Money moved its attention from prices to the size of the next rate rise. Cash left Treasuries on the rate outlook, lifting the real yield and the dollar. Gold, freed last week from the headwind of oil, met the heavier headwind of the real yield and broke $4,000. Equities rose on cheaper oil and an AI rebound, moving the other way from gold.**

The start of the week still belonged to the previous one. On 11/9 President Trump said a fresh strike on Iran was unlikely as long as talks continued, and WTI fell 4.38% to 86.85 (CNBC). The dollar index slipped to 101.69 (reading) and gold rose 1.73% to 4,141 (reading), above its intraday high of 11/5. The S&P 500 gained 2.35%, its largest daily rise since April.

What changed the direction was US October CPI on 11/10. The headline rose 0.4% on the month (0.3% expected) and the core 0.3% (0.2% expected), the third straight month of 0.3% at the core (CNBC). The 2-year rose 17bp to 4.73% and the real yield 12bp to 2.78%; gold gave back the previous day's gain in a single session, down 2.61%. The Treasury market was closed on 11/11, so the dollar was bought first and gold broke 4,000. When the market reopened on 11/12 it priced two days at once: the 2-year reached 4.86% and the 10-year 5.10%, clearing 5%. Gold fell 2.81% to 3,876 (reading), and Kitco called that low the lowest in 13 months.

**Over the week the 10-year rose 29bp, of which 28bp was the real yield and 1bp expected inflation.** The previous week it was expected inflation that carried nominal yields, falling with oil. This week the market read higher prices as something the Fed would hold down with rate rises, and the real yield moved. Derived from fed funds futures, December went from 0.76 moves the previous week to 1.10, pricing more than a full 0.25%. Before Friday's PPI, Kitco reported odds of 89% for 0.25% and 11% for 0.5%.

US October PPI on Friday rose 0.3% on the month, below the 0.4% expected. The year-on-year rate was 6.5%, the highest since November 2022, and the index excluding food, energy and trade was a strong 0.5% (Kitco). Gold recovered to the 3,897 area after PPI, but the 10-year touched 5.17% (Yahoo Finance) and gold settled at 3,883, up 0.18%, by the time of the PM report (Kitco). **A single softer print did not lower the outlook for rates.**

Set against last week's scenarios, **the close ran well past the bear objective (3,985 after breaking 4,000, then 3,969).** The triggers, core CPI at 0.3% or more on the month and a December rise being treated as certain, both happened. What did not happen was "selling to cover equity losses": equities rose. What pushed gold down was the real yield and the dollar, not forced selling.

## Key events, reviewed

### 1. A hot US October CPI takes December beyond one full move (11/10 to 11/12)

US October CPI on 11/10 came in 0.1 point above expectations at both the headline (0.4% on the month) and the core (0.3%), per CNBC and Morningstar. The BLS does not calculate the year-on-year rate. The probability of a 0.5% rise in December was still small at 13.6% (CNBC, CME FedWatch).

The next day, 11/11, was Veterans Day in the US and the bond market was closed. Only equities and the dollar traded: the dollar index rose to 102.92 (reading) and USD/JPY reached 159.78, the weakest yen in 10 weeks. When Treasuries reopened on 11/12 they were sold in bulk, taking the 2-year to 4.86% (+13bp against 11/10), the 10-year to 5.10% (+12bp) and the real yield to 2.86% (+8bp), per the Treasury. San Francisco Fed President Daly said her view was that further tightening would be needed (Yahoo Finance), and New York Fed President Williams also indicated more action was required (Kitco).

→ **CPI moved the argument from whether the Fed acts to how far it goes.** Fed funds futures price 1.10 moves for December and a cumulative rise of about 46bp through the January meeting. That is a large shift from 0.76 moves the previous week, after the payrolls report and this inflation print.

### 2. Gold falls three days running and breaks $4,000 (11/10 to 11/13)

On the USAGOLD reading, gold fell three days in a row: from 4,141 on 11/9 to 4,033 on 11/10 (−2.61%), 3,993 on 11/11 (−0.97%) and 3,876 on 11/12 (−2.81%). Intraday on 11/12 it reached close to 3,852 (investingLive). Silver rose on 11/11 while gold fell, but lost 5.35% on the week, more than gold, and broke 50.

Friday brought dip buying on the soft PPI: Kitco's morning piece had gold in the 3,891 area, recovering to the 3,897 area after the release. It stopped short of 3,903, Kitco's first resistance, and ended the week at 3,884.15 on the USAGOLD reading. Silver was sold again on Friday, with spot down 2.63%; Kitco pointed to worries about industrial demand and tighter financial conditions.

→ **Gold's fall tracked the rate outlook closely.** The two large falls, on 11/10 and 11/12, land on the two days when the 2-year rose 17bp and 13bp. When PPI came in soft on Friday the 2-year held at 4.88% rather than falling, and gold's recovery was small to match.

### 3. Equities rise on cheaper oil and an AI rebound, with Nvidia's CEO exit weighing late (11/9 to 11/13)

Oil fell on 11/9 after Trump's remarks on Iran, and the S&P 500 rose 2.35% with the semiconductor index up 6.85%, a strong rebound from the previous week's plunge. On 11/11 CoreWeave's results sent its shares up 24.71%, with the S&P 500 up 1.31% and the semiconductor index up 5.66%. In Tokyo the Nikkei 225 recovered from 57,434.77 on 11/6 to close the week up 6.46% at 61,142.35, back above 60,000.

The second half of the week was weighed down by Nvidia's announcement on 11/12. Jensen Huang resigned as president, CEO and director with immediate effect, with CFO Colette Kress serving as interim CEO (TechCrunch, CNN). The shares fell more than 13% intraday on 11/12 to close down 4.34%, and ended Friday down 2.01% at 162.85 after a downgrade from HSBC, a fall of about 12% on the week that took the market value below 4 trillion dollars (Yahoo Finance). On Friday the S&P 500 fell 0.40% and the Nasdaq 0.91%, with only the Dow up 0.1%.

## How money moved this week

**Treasuries → sold as the size of the move was priced ／ Dollar → bought on rate differentials ／ Gold and silver → sold on the rising real yield ／ Equities → bought back on cheaper oil and an AI rebound**

The first flow was **money leaving Treasuries on the rate outlook**. The 2-year rose 37bp and the 10-year 29bp; shorter maturities rose more and the 2s10s spread narrowed 8bp. Of the 10-year's rise, 28bp was the real yield and only 1bp expected inflation. Fed funds futures took December from 0.76 moves to 1.10, pricing more than a full 0.25%. The MOVE index rose 6.49% while the VIX fell 24.73%: this week's caution sat in bonds, not equities.

The second flow was **money leaving gold and silver**. Gold fell 4.58%, silver 5.35% and platinum 3.23%, the precious metals falling together. The gold/silver ratio rose slightly from 78.7 to 79.3, so this was not fear buying lifting gold alone but the rate outlook selling the metals as a group. The gold/oil ratio fell from 44.9 to 43.9 and the gold/copper ratio from 655 to 639: gold fell further than either oil or copper. **What rates sold was the asset that pays no interest.** Over the last 8 weeks the correlation of gold with the real yield is −0.25 and with the dollar index −0.26, turning clearly negative from near zero the previous week. Speculative length in gold was still high at about 104 thousand contracts as of 11/3, the latest data; how far positions were cut in the fall after CPI will show in the coming weeks' releases.

Third, **the buying back of equities continued**. The S&P 500 rose 2.51% and the Nikkei 225 6.46%, recovering the previous week's plunge. Oil fell 2.50% for a second straight week and the US high-yield spread narrowed 22bp. Even with yields rising, selling did not spread to corporate credit, and equities took higher rates as the flip side of a strong economy. BTC, however, fell 6.10% against the equity market, with spot ETFs seeing outflows five days running for 1.065 billion dollars on the week. It briefly broke 70,000 on Friday (Yahoo Finance). What rates sold was, again, the side that pays no interest.

→ In summary: a hot CPI sent the market to price the size of December's move. Treasuries were sold, the real yield and the dollar rose, and money left gold, silver and BTC, none of which pay interest. Equities were bought on cheaper oil and an AI rebound, and credit shows no flight either. What pushed gold down was rates, not forced selling.

Japan's first estimate of Q3 GDP lands on 11/16. Private forecasts are split, from −0.4% annualised on the quarter (Dai-ichi Life Research Institute) to +1.0% (Daiwa Institute of Research). A contraction would be a headwind for the view that the Bank of Japan raises rates in December.

### Geopolitics

The US continues its naval blockade of Iran, and on 11/12 President Trump posted that no deal with Iran was needed (Kitco). While there is no sign of resuming large-scale military action, attacks by the pro-Iran Houthis on Saudi energy facilities and threats to tankers continue (Kitco).
