## The core theme of the week

**It was a week in which the bill for higher yields went only to gold, small caps and crypto.**

The US 10-year yield reached 5.23% at one point on Friday 9/25, up from 5.004% the previous Friday, the highest since June 2007. On 9/24 the 30-year hit 5.501%, the highest since 2004, and the Japanese 10-year reached 3.075%, its highest since 1996. This was not a week about the rate outlook in one country: government bonds across the developed world were repriced together.

And yet equities finished higher. The S&P 500 rose 1.2% on the week, the Nasdaq Composite 2.1% and the Nikkei 225 also 2.1%. The Dow jumped 479 dollars on Friday for its first weekly gain in 4 weeks. Gold, by contrast, fell about 2%, and the Russell 2000 and BTC were sold hardest in the middle of the week.

Facing the same shock in yields, **the equity indices absorbed it through concentration in semiconductors and AI, and only the assets that pay no interest and the assets that depend on borrowing fell straightforwardly.** The chain of cheaper oil, then lower yields, then higher equities that held in the first half of the week reversed on 9/23 on the rebound in oil and remarks from Fed officials, and did not come back before the weekend.

Money　US and Japanese government bonds → the dollar and short-term rates

## Key events, reviewed

### 1. Oil falls, rebounds, and then the Hormuz talks (9/21 to 9/25)

At the start of the week President Trump's openness to talking to Iran and higher Saudi exports sent WTI down for a fourth straight day, below 94 dollars. Inflation expectations came off and the US 10-year fell below 5%, to 4.95% on 9/21. That was the starting point for the equity gains in the first half of the week.

On 9/23 oil rebounded for the first time in 5 days on speculation about diesel export restrictions, and the force pushing yields down disappeared. On Friday it was reported that the US and Iran were discussing a phased reopening of the Strait of Hormuz in New York, and WTI closed at 92.41 dollars, down 2.33%. Attacks by the Houthis on Riyadh and Aramco's Yanbu facility continued, however, and Brent rose on the week instead, into the 104 dollar area.

→ The chain from cheaper oil to lower yields lasted only through the first half of the week. **That the gap between WTI and Brent widened beyond 12 dollars** shows not that supply risk in the Middle East has been resolved, but that pricing has split between the US market and the Middle East.

### 2. Hawkish remarks from Fed officials, and the simultaneous selloff in US and Japanese government bonds (9/22 to 9/25)

Even after the first rate rise in 3 years on 9/16, to 3.75% to 4.00%, Fed officials did not soften their hawkish line. Remarks by Governor Barr on 9/23 lifted expectations of an October rate rise to around 70%, and on 9/24 three officials, including New York Fed President Williams, all spoke in favour of further tightening. The probability of an October rise seen in CME FedWatch rose to about 73%.

Strong US PMI readings for September came on top of this, so the rise in yields was supported both by inflation expectations and by the strength of the economy. The Japanese 10-year was dragged up with them, adding 10bp to 3.075% on 9/24. The final University of Michigan consumer sentiment reading on Friday stayed low at 48.1, but one-year inflation expectations were high at 4.6%, so it was not material that would push yields down.

→ The US 10-year touched 5.23% on Friday before closing around 5.17%. **That is a rise of about 16bp on the week**, and it was the starting point for every movement of money in the week.

### 3. Oracle's force majeure notice and Friday's turnaround in equities (9/24 to 9/25)

On 9/24 it was reported that Oracle had sent a force majeure notice to counterparties over the 16.5 billion dollar data centre project it is pursuing with OpenAI and SoftBank, and its shares fell. From that day the market added a concern on a different track from yields: the funding of AI capital spending.

Even so, equities did not break on Friday. The S&P 500 closed at 7,743.41, up 0.51%, the Nasdaq Composite at 27,068.72, up 0.5%, and the Dow at 51,828.62, up 478.64 dollars. Semiconductors were strong, and the indices rose together on a day when the 10-year printed in the 5.2% area. The Nikkei 225 also rose for a fifth straight day on Friday, adding 850.21 yen to 66,364.20, with TOPIX up 1.31% alongside it.

→ **Equities judged that the rise in yields was not enough to damage earnings.** The credit question that starts with Oracle, however, was not erased by Friday's gains, and it carries over into next week and beyond.

## How money moved this week

**Oil → US Treasuries → the dollar and short-term rates ／ Gold, small caps and BTC → semiconductors and AI**

The first flow was **a temporary move out of oil and into US Treasuries, and then its unwinding**. At the start of the week the risk premium came off oil, and money went into bonds as inflation expectations retreated (the US 10-year at 4.95%). When oil rebounded on 9/23 and the Fed piled on hawkish remarks, bonds were sold instead and money moved to the dollar and short-term rates. The US 10-year at around 5.17% at the end of the week, and the DXY in its highest area since late July, are where that landed.

Money　US Treasuries → the dollar and short-term rates

The second flow was **outflows from assets that pay no interest and assets that are weak to interest**. Gold fell about 2% on the week and broke below 4,300 dollars. On 9/23 equities fell across the board, and the size of the falls ran in the order Dow, S&P, Nasdaq, Russell 2000, exactly the order of sensitivity to yields. BTC was bought up into the 87,000 dollar area early in the week, then pushed back to around 84,000 dollars as yields rose. On 9/22 gold fell while BTC rose; from 9/23 onwards both fell. **BTC behaved as a risk asset weak to yields, not as a hedge against dilution.**

Money　Gold, small caps and BTC → the dollar and short-term rates

Third, **within equities, concentration moved from financial shares to semiconductors and AI**. On 9/22, the day the Nasdaq set a record high, JPMorgan and Bank of America fell more than 3%. The cause was not yields but a problem of their own, the slowdown in investment banking fees. What held up the indices was a single theme, semiconductors, and Friday's turnaround was also led by semiconductors. Oracle's force majeure notice showed, in concrete form for the first time, the weak point this concentration carries: the funding of AI capital spending.

Money　Financial shares → semiconductors and AI-related shares

One more move on the Japanese side is worth recording. USD/JPY weakened to 158.96 on 9/24, then recovered about 1% on Friday as the Japanese government talked against yen weakness, ending the week in the low 157s. Even with the Japanese 10-year at levels last seen in 1996 the yen was not bought; what moved the yen was not the rate differential but remarks from the authorities.

→ In summary: in a week when government bonds were sold worldwide, equities as indices rose and gold fell. **The pain of higher yields was not handed out evenly across assets; it concentrated on the things that pay no interest and the things that rely on borrowing.**

## Looking to next week

### The event that matters most

**US August PCE on Wednesday 9/30, and the US September jobs report on Friday 10/2.** Ahead of the FOMC on October 27 and 28, the material that decides how certain an October rate rise is (priced at about 73%) is concentrated in these two. If either comes in hot, the US 10-year will go on to test this week's high of 5.23%.

The US ISM manufacturing index on Thursday 10/1 is the reading that checks the other side of what supported the rise in yields this week, the strength of the economy. The Bank of Japan's Tankan survey comes out on the morning of the same day.

One further note: the risk of a US government shutdown at the 9/30 deadline is not material for next week, because a stopgap budget running to December 11 was passed at the start of September.

### Domestic politics and policy

The largest focus is how effective the talk against yen weakness is. The yen recovered about 1% on Friday on remarks from the Japanese government, but the rate differential between the US and Japan has not narrowed, and if US data come in hot a return towards the upper 158s is possible. Japan has said it will maintain the stance of the intervention it carried out jointly with the US in July, so **how far the remarks are backed by actual money will decide the upper end of the yen rate.**

The Tankan survey on 10/1 is the material for checking how willing companies are to pass on prices and to invest, at a point when the Japanese 10-year sits in the 3% area.

### Geopolitics

If the talks between the US and Iran on a phased reopening of the Strait of Hormuz progress, more of Brent's risk premium comes off, which eases the upward pressure on yields through inflation expectations. That is the path that reproduces the chain of cheaper oil, then lower yields, then higher equities from the first half of this week.

Conversely, if the Houthi attacks on Saudi facilities continue and the talks stall, Brent stays in this week's higher area and the gap of more than 12 dollars to WTI does not narrow. On this week's experience, **the rebound in oil worked as a headwind for gold, through the rise in yields.** It is worth noting that geopolitical tension is not necessarily a tailwind for gold.

### Nikkei and the yen

The Nikkei 225 rose for a fifth straight day to 66,364 yen, consolidating above 65,000 for the first time since September 10. That TOPIX also rose 1.31% on Friday means the advance broadened, an improvement on the previous week, when only the Nikkei rose and TOPIX fell. The Nikkei VI, on the other hand, jumped to 26.62 on 9/24, so gains and caution sit side by side.

Next week the focus is whether Friday's stronger yen (the low 157s) bites into exporters, and the direction of US yields after the US data.

Expected range: high 67,500 / low 64,800

The range is the editor's assumption. The upper bound assumes this week's gain (about 1,350 yen) extends again next week, and the lower bound is set as a level that clearly breaks the 65,000 area seen at the start of the week.
