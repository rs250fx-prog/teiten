## The core theme of the week

**It was a week in which long yields tested a ceiling and were pushed back each time, and the source of those swings sat outside the United States.**

After closing at 5.31% on 10/5, the US 10-year yield rose intraday on 10/7 and 10/8 to around 5.35% to 5.36%, the highest since 2002. Each time, though, it came back by the close, and the week ended at 5.24%, 4bp below the previous Friday's 5.28%. On the Treasury's confirmed values the real yield went from 2.92% to 2.91%, the 2-year from 4.83% to 4.80% and the 30-year from 5.63% to 5.60%: **the change across the week was small at every maturity. What was large was the swing inside it.**

There were three sources of that swing. European fiscal worries: the gap between French and German government bond yields widened to its largest since 2011, and the UK 30-year reached 6.047%, the highest since 1998. The FOMC minutes of 10/7, in which most participants expected one more rate rise this year. And Middle East oil, with the 10/8 report that a strike on Iran was under consideration. **It was not US price data but fiscal and geopolitical news from outside the country that moved US long yields intraday.**

Set against last week's scenarios (week 40), the close landed on base, the 4,111 to 4,230 range. Part of the bear trigger did happen intraday, however: long yields cleared the 5.344% high of 10/1, and gold fell on 10/7 to 4,110.82 dollars, the edge of bear. Even so it did not clearly break 4,111 dollars, and it recovered to 4,194 on Friday. **The material for a headwind appeared, but closing yields did not follow it, so gold did not break down.**

Money　European bonds → sold → the euro → the dollar ／ US long bonds → sold intraday, bought back at the close ／ Gold → dip buying at 4,111 dollars

## Key events, reviewed

### 1. European fiscal and political worries, and a fourth straight weekly rise in the dollar index (10/5 to 10/9)

At the start of the week, on 10/5, the euro hit a 17-month low against the dollar on French fiscal worries and the dissolution of parliament for a general election in Spain, and the dollar index rose to 102.24 (102.54 intraday). The gap between French and German bond yields was reported to have widened to about 160bp, the widest since 2011. On 10/6 the French government set out a plan to cut the fiscal deficit through spending restraint and tax rises, the yield gap narrowed and the dollar index fell into the upper 101s, but by 10/7 it was back at 102.25 along with the minutes.

→ The dollar index rose about 0.3% on the week for a fourth straight weekly gain, the longest run since May 2025 according to Reuters. **The US 10-year yield fell on the week, yet the dollar rose.** The main driver of dollar strength was not US rates but selling of the euro. As of Friday the euro was still around 1.12 dollars, reported down about 0.5% on the week.

### 2. The FOMC minutes and the intraday spike in US long yields (10/7 to 10/8)

The minutes of the 9/15 to 9/16 meeting, released on 10/7, record that the 0.25 point rise (to 3.75% to 4.00%) was backed by every participant and that most of them saw one more rise this year as likely to be appropriate. The US 10-year rose intraday to around 5.36% on 10/7 and close to 5.35% on 10/8, but closed at 5.28% and 5.22%. A solid auction result was reported to have eased yields on 10/7, and on 10/8 yields came back along with oil after Trump posted that he would not strike Iran before the midterm elections.

On Friday the 10-year rose slightly to 5.24% (+2bp) and the real yield to 2.91% (+4bp). The University of Michigan's October consumer sentiment reading (preliminary) was 46.3, the lowest since May, and inflation expectations rose to 4.7% at one year ahead (from 4.6% the month before) and to 3.5% at five years (from 3.4%). St. Louis Fed President Musalem said another rate rise would be needed to bring inflation back to 2%.

→ On CME FedWatch the probability of a rise in October was 19%, and of at least one rise by December 84% (Reuters). That is close to the previous Friday: **the pricing of rate rises barely moved this week.** What moved was only the intraday level of long yields, a swing with no change in the policy outlook behind it.

## How money moved this week

**European bonds and the euro → the dollar ／ US long bonds sold intraday and bought back at the close ／ AI shares → sold for a time, gold → dip buying at 4,111 dollars**

The first flow was **money moving from Europe into the dollar**. The euro was sold on France's finances and Spain's election, and the dollar index rose for a fourth straight week. That the dollar rose in a week when the US 10-year yield fell 4bp shows that the main driver of dollar strength has moved from US rates to European fiscal worries. For gold, the headwind from the dollar continues along a path separate from US data.

Money　European bonds and the euro → sold ／ The dollar → bought

The second flow was **selling of US long bonds intraday and buying them back into the close**. Yields were pushed to around 5.35% to 5.36% intraday on 10/7 and 10/8 but came back by the close, leaving the week 4bp lower. The real yield peaked for the week at 2.95% (10/5), fell to 2.87% (10/8) and ended at 2.91%. Breakeven inflation (the 10-year BEI) did not move, holding between 2.33% and 2.36%. **There are sellers of long bonds, but in the 5.3% range buyers appear as well.** Last week's 5.344% and this week's 5.36% area now line up as levels that closes could not clear, which includes the editor's assumption.

Money　US long bonds → sold intraday, bought back at the close ／ The real yield → close to flat

Third, **inside risk assets, AI-related names and small caps were sold in turn.** On 10/7 it was small caps on rising yields, and on 10/8 the Nasdaq on the OpenAI report: the place being sold changed from day to day, and the indices still rose on the week. BTC, meanwhile, fell 2.3%, from 84,506 dollars (10/3, 09:00 JST) to 82,542 dollars (10/10, 09:03 JST), and dropped to 81,686 on the morning of 10/9 (CoinGecko). **Gold rose 1.2% and BTC fell 2.3%: the two alternative assets went opposite ways this week.** BTC fell hard on the day the Nasdaq plunged, while gold rose on the day yields fell and on the day of bargain hunting.

Money　AI-related and small-cap shares → sold, changing by the day ／ BTC → sold ／ Gold → dip buying

→ In summary: long yields tested a 24-year high intraday and were pushed back by the close each time, leaving the week unchanged. **What moved asset prices was not the level of yields but the intraday swings brought by European finances and Middle East headlines**, and through them gold defended 4,111 dollars for a second week running.

## Looking to next week

### The event that matters most

**US September CPI on Wednesday 10/14.** Forecasts are +0.6% on the month (from +0.4%) and 3.6% year on year (from 3.4%), with the core at +0.2% on the month and 2.5% year on year (investingLive, Newsquawk; some forecasts put the year-on-year rate at 3.7%). The headline is expected to accelerate, reflecting higher oil, so the focus is the core and how it squares with inflation expectations, which rose to 4.7% in the University of Michigan survey. Retail sales (+0.3% expected) and PPI (+0.5%) follow on Thursday 10/15, and the third-quarter earnings season starts with the US bank results on 10/13 and 10/14.

Long yields swung on news from abroad this week, but next week becomes **a week in which US prices move US yields on news of their own**. Ahead of the FOMC on 27 and 28 October, the point to read in CPI is whether the 19% priced for an October rise moves. On Monday 10/12 the US bond market is closed for Columbus Day and Japan is closed for Sports Day, so the week starts thin.

### Domestic politics and policy

The focus is the timing of the Bank of Japan's next rate rise and USD/JPY in the 159s. The next BOJ meeting is on 10/29 and 10/30, with the Outlook Report. Pricing for an October rise ranges by source from around 20% to about a quarter. Next week brings remarks from board member Koeda on Thursday 10/15 and an address by Governor Ueda, read on his behalf by Deputy Governor Uchida, on Friday 10/16, both of which give clues on October versus December.

USD/JPY held a range of about 1 yen this week, 157.43 to 158.50, and was pushed back short of the 200-day line (158.54) on 10/7. One view is that in the 159s remarks from the US Treasury Secretary pushing back on a weak yen need watching. The G20 finance ministers and central bank governors meet in Bangkok on Thursday 10/15, and any reference to currencies could be material. We could not confirm any domestic political development that moved the market this week.

### Geopolitics

In the Strait of Hormuz, 9 tankers were attacked over the past week, and crude leaving the strait is running at about 9.5 million barrels a day, roughly 30% below the pre-war level (Kpler data, via CNBC). On 10/8 WTI rose nearly 5% intraday on reports that the US was considering a large-scale military operation against Iran, then pared the gain after Trump posted that he would not strike Iran before the midterm elections on 3 November.

**With a deadline for not striking now stated, the risk of military action has receded until 3 November, while the damage to supply from attacks on ships in the strait continues.** Again this week, the rise in oil came in the same hours as the intraday rise in long yields. If tanker attacks increase and Brent clears 105 dollars, the chain of higher oil → higher long yields → a headwind for gold starts moving; if traffic through the strait recovers, one weight on yields comes off. The OPEC monthly report on 10/13 and the IEA oil market report on 10/14 make it a week for checking the supply and demand outlook.
