## The core theme of the week

**Money fled from AI-related shares into Treasuries. Gold and silver were not the refuge; they were on the side being turned into cash to cover losses in equities. That is the same picture for a third week running.**

The first half of the week began with a recovery. Anthropic's filing to list on the Nasdaq brought buyers back to AI-related shares, and the S&P 500 rose for three days running through 10/28 (Yahoo Finance). Gold too regained the 4,000 dollar area, on confirmed USAGOLD values of 4,017.08, then 4,060.38, then 4,067.55. The direction changed from 10/29. Alphabet fell 12.6% on its results, the largest fall since it listed (Yahoo Finance), and on Friday Amazon's and Intel's results were sold one after the other. Intel fell about 29% and the Nasdaq 2.86%, its largest fall since June, with October's monthly fall exceeding 5% (Yahoo Finance).

Money fled into Treasuries. On the week the US 10-year fell from 5.00% to 4.94%, the 2-year from 4.69% to 4.56% and the real yield from 2.66% to 2.60%, while the dollar index was flat at 101.88 (Treasury, Yahoo Finance). **That is a tailwind week for gold. Even so gold fell 3.03%, from 3,996.18 to 3,875.20 dollars (reading), and Kitco called it a one-year low.** Silver lost 8.66% and platinum 7.61%, both sold harder than gold. Kitco wrote of Friday's fall that it came while Treasury yields were falling, and that the weakness in equities had intensified liquidation across the precious metals.

Set against the previous week's (week 43) scenarios, **the first half of the week reached the top of the base case (4,068) and the close ran below even the bear objective (3,900 after breaking 3,950).** Among the bear triggers, though, PCE came in as expected and the VIX, in the 19s, did not clear the 22.31 of 10/16. The one that landed was "results spread worry about the returns on AI investment", and that alone was enough to reach the objective. The previous week's view took too lightly that gold can be sold on equity weakness alone, without the conditions in rates and inflation lining up.

Money　AI-related shares → sold (Nasdaq −2.86% on Friday) → Treasuries → bought (2-year −13bp) ／ Oil → bought (WTI 104.12 dollars) ／ Gold, silver and BTC → sold to raise cash (gold −3.03%, silver −8.66%)

## Key events, reviewed

### 1. AI-related results are sold for three days running (10/28 to 10/30)

Alphabet's July–September results, released after the close on 10/28, showed Google Cloud revenue growing but profit short of expectations, and on 10/29 the shares fell 12.64%, the largest fall since the 2004 listing (Yahoo Finance). After the close the same day, Amazon missed on both revenue and profit, and its October–December revenue outlook also came in below expectations (Yahoo Finance).

On Friday Amazon fell about 2% and Intel about 29%, its worst day in decades, after its October–December outlook fell short (Yahoo Finance, StockTitan). The Nasdaq fell 2.86%, the S&P 500 1.82% and the Dow 1.09%, as the market sorted, results by results, whether the weight of AI investment turns into profit. As the rise in small caps on 10/29 showed, this is not flight from equities as a whole but a continuing unwind of the concentration in large AI-related names.

Tokyo followed on Friday. The Nikkei 225 had risen three days running to 64,659.47 on 10/29, but on Friday it fell 1,369.36 points (2.12%) to 63,290.11, down more than 1,900 points at one stage (Gaitame.com). Semiconductor-related names led the selling, and the Nikkei VI jumped from 25.80 to 33.82.

→ **The sorting is not over.** The Nasdaq fell 2.00% on the week, a third straight weekly fall, and Friday's drop carries over into Tokyo the following week.

### 2. Gold hits a one-year low and silver falls more than 8%: liquidation inside a tailwind (10/26 to 10/30)

On confirmed USAGOLD values gold was 4,017.08 dollars on 10/26, 4,060.38 on 10/27 and 4,067.55 on 10/28, recovering about 40% of the previous week's fall in the first half of the week. On 10/29 it fell to 4,025.63 (reading) on a stronger GDP print and Alphabet's plunge, and on Friday it fell more than 3% in a single day. Kitco's afternoon report had spot at 3,879.90 (−3.52%) and silver at 49.015 (−6.06%), with gold breaking 3,900 to a one-year low.

In the morning Kitco wrote that higher oil from the tanker attack in the Strait of Hormuz had strengthened the view on inflation and a further rate rise; in the afternoon it wrote that the weakness in equities had intensified liquidation across the precious metals. The FOMC held on 10/28 by 10 votes to 2, with Governor Cook and President Hammack arguing for a rise. On Friday St. Louis Fed President Musalem touched on the need to move early, and Richmond Fed President Barkin also said a further rise was possible (Yahoo Finance).

→ **Treasuries were bought all the same, and the 2-year fell 13bp on the week.** The view on rate rises worked to push gold down, but rates themselves moved the other way. What took gold lower was not rates but selling to make cash on days when equities fell. Silver and platinum falling 2 to 3 times as much as gold points the same way: the assets that had risen most were sold hardest.

### 3. The FOMC and the Bank of Japan hold, and a tanker is attacked in Hormuz (10/28 to 10/30)

The FOMC on 10/28 held the policy rate at 3.75% to 4.00%, and Chair Warsh gave no guidance on the path ahead. July–September GDP, released on 10/29, rose at an annualised 3.4% against the 3.1% expected, and September PCE was 3.6% on the year, in line (Kitco, Yahoo Finance). The ECB also held its deposit rate at 2.5% on 10/29.

The Bank of Japan held its policy rate at around 1.25% on 10/30. The vote was 8 to 1, with board member Takata proposing a rise to 1.50% and being outvoted. In its Outlook Report it judged the underlying rate of inflation to be at a level "broadly consistent" with the 2% target, and from January 2027 it will also speed up its ETF sales, from about 330 billion yen a year to about 860 billion yen at book value (Nikkei, Gaitame.com). Governor Ueda said that sustained and stable achievement of 2% had "not yet been reached", and USD/JPY fell to 157.12 right after the announcement before recovering (Gaitame.com).

Oil fell in the first half of the week after Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan held talks with Iran in Tehran, and WTI broke 100 dollars, to 99.90, on 10/27. It recovered on 10/28 on reports that the US had told Gulf states it was preparing to strike Iran, and on Friday a merchant tanker in the Strait of Hormuz was attacked with explosives attached from a small boat (Kitco, Reuters). WTI ended the week up 0.82% at 104.12 and Brent up 0.79% at 117.64, with October the fourth straight monthly gain (Reuters, via The Globe and Mail).

→ **None of the three central banks moved, and only the signal of tightening was left.** In December a rate rise is the question for both the Fed and the Bank of Japan, and higher oil supports that view.

## How money moved this week

**AI-related shares → into Treasuries ／ Oil → bought on the attack while speculators step back ／ Gold and silver → the source of cash inside a tailwind**

The first flow was **flight from AI-related shares into Treasuries**. The 2-year fell more than the 10-year, and the 2s10s curve steepened 7bp to +38bp. Fed funds futures price 0.70 moves for December and 2 FOMC members argued for a rise, yet the shorter maturities were bought hardest. This was a week when flight from equity weakness beat the view on rates. The US high-yield spread, meanwhile, narrowed 12bp as of 10/29, so selling has not spread to corporate credit. **This is a rotation inside equities, not risk-off that carries credit stress.** Spot BTC ETFs also saw 202 million dollars of outflows on the week, a small withdrawal from risk assets in general.

The second flow was **the gap between oil's rise and speculators leaving**. WTI ended the week in the 104 dollar area, but in the CFTC positioning data managed money's net length fell for a fourth straight week, from about 54 thousand contracts on 10/6 to about 31 thousand on 10/27. What is lifting the price is not speculative buying but supply worry that rises with each attack. The gold/oil ratio falling from 38.8 to 37.4 points the same way: the factors in oil were the stronger.

Third, **selling gold and silver to raise cash**. The gold/silver ratio rose sharply from 74.6 to 79.4, not because gold was bought but because silver was sold. In the positioning data, speculative net length in silver fell for a fourth straight week, to about 3,300 contracts on 10/27, with buying all but exhausted. Net length in gold rose slightly, to about 106 thousand contracts on 10/27, but Friday's plunge came after that. Over the last 8 weeks the correlation of gold with the real yield is −0.02 and with the dollar index +0.06, near zero either way: **gold is now moving on the needs of equity positions, not on rates or the dollar.**

→ In summary: the sorting within AI-related shares sent buyers into Treasuries, and rates and the dollar turned into a tailwind for gold. Gold was sold all the same, as the source of cash to cover equity losses, and silver was sold harder still. Only oil rose, on the attack, and it supports the view on a December rate rise.

## Looking to next week

### The event that matters most

The heaviest item is **the US October employment report on 11/6**. Nonfarm payrolls are expected at 55 thousand (29 thousand last time), the unemployment rate at 4.2% and average hourly earnings up 0.3% on the month (Newsquawk). Expectations range from 55 thousand to 65 thousand depending on the source (CMC Markets, FXStreet). December is priced at 0.70 moves on our calculation; weakness in employment would lower that, and strong wages would push it towards being treated as certain.

Before that come the ISM manufacturing index on 11/2 (54.7 expected) and the ISM non-manufacturing index on 11/4 (54.7 expected). The price components are the focus in both. The US Treasury's quarterly refunding announcement also lands on 11/4; a larger issue size at the longer maturities would be a reason for the 10-year yield, pushed down by flight, to come back.

Abroad, the question is whether the RBA on 11/3 and the BoE on 11/5 raise rates, and Newsquawk expects a 0.25% rise from both. Some also expect the BoE to hold (CMC Markets), so views are split.

### Domestic politics and policy

Tokyo is closed for Culture Day on 11/3, so the midterm results are taken in Tokyo on 11/4. The Bank of Japan judged underlying inflation to be "broadly consistent" with 2%, and the market's attention has moved to a rise to 1.50% in December (Gaitame.com). The minutes of the September meeting, on 11/5, will show the substance of the discussion that decided September's rise.

The decision to speed up ETF sales by about 2.6 times from January 2027 matters not for supply and demand now but as a reason for the support under equities to thin out next year. USD/JPY is in the 157s, and the Ministry of Finance held back from stronger language even when the yen reached the 162s in June (Bloomberg, via Yahoo! Finance Japan). At the current level we take the Bank of Japan's December decision, rather than the watch for intervention, as the thing that moves the yen.

### Geopolitics

The war between the US and Israel and Iran has entered its eighth month, and a tanker was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz again on Friday. Who was responsible has not been established (Kitco). There are also reports of a proposal mediated by Qatar to end the war (The Globe and Mail), so talks and attacks continue side by side.

**The midterm elections on 11/3 are the dividing line.** Since the previous week there have been reports of a plan to resume strikes on Iran after the vote. Polls point to the possibility that the majority in the House changes hands (investingLive). If military action follows the election, the chain of headwinds for gold starts to turn: higher oil → higher expected inflation → a December rise treated as certain → the fall in the real yield stops. If talks progress instead, cheaper oil lowers the view on rate rises and that works as a tailwind for gold.
